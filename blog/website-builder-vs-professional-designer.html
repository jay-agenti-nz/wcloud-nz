Website builder or web designer? What small NZ businesses should actually weigh up

Most guides comparing website builders to hiring a professional are written by one side or the other, so they’re not that useful. Here’s a more honest version.

What a website builder is actually good at

Wix, Squarespace and similar tools are genuinely good products. If you’re comfortable spending a weekend on it, you can get a reasonable-looking site live for the cost of a monthly subscription. That’s a fair trade for some businesses, especially very early on.

Where builders tend to fall short isn’t the tool — it’s everything around the tool:

  • The blank page problem. Most people don’t struggle with the software. They struggle with what to actually say about their own business, in what order, in a way that makes someone want to enquire.
  • Time. “A weekend” often becomes several weekends, spread over a month, competing with actually running the business.
  • SEO basics get skipped. Builders can support good SEO, but the default settings rarely have it configured properly — someone still has to do that work.
  • It’s easy to end up with something that looks like a template, because it is one, and customers have seen the same layout on ten other small business sites.

What paying someone actually buys you

Not magic — just someone else doing the parts you’d otherwise have to learn or muddle through:

  • The words written for you, in a way that’s actually about your business.
  • Design decisions made by someone who’s made them before, so the site doesn’t read as generic.
  • Basic SEO set up correctly from day one, instead of guessed at.
  • Your time back, to spend on the business instead of the website.

When DIY genuinely makes more sense

Being honest about the other side too:

  • You’re testing an idea and don’t yet know if the business will stick around.
  • You genuinely enjoy this kind of thing and have the time to spend on it.
  • Your needs are extremely simple — a single page with your contact details is fine.
  • Budget is the deciding factor and $0–$20/month is what you can spend right now.

There’s no shame in any of that. Plenty of businesses start on a builder and move to something more built-out once they know the business is working.

The middle ground people don’t always know exists

The choice isn’t only “$20/month DIY” vs “$8,000 agency”. There’s a real middle option: a professional builds the site for you, at a price closer to a builder subscription than a full agency project, without the months-long process. If you want the detail on what each price bracket buys, we broke it down in what a website actually costs in New Zealand.

A quick way to decide

Ask yourself one question: is the time you’d spend building it yourself worth more to your business than the cost of paying someone to do it? For most small business owners, once you actually total up the hours, the answer becomes pretty clear.
