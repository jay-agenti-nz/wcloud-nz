Why I shoot

I’d be out there with a camera anyway.

Jay D Vera

Most weekends I’m somewhere on the Port Hills — Godley Head, Taylors Mistake, Scarborough Bluffs — running the trails and filming them for my Join me… series. Nobody asked me to make those. I make them because I like the problem: how do you make someone feel a place they’ve never stood in? Get that right and everything else is just settings.

The other half of me is ten years running creative and e-commerce brands, and that’s the part that taught me the difference between a photo that looks good and a photo that gets used. When you tell me you need something for Instagram this week, I know what you actually mean — frames you can post on a Tuesday without redesigning your whole feed, and a clip that holds a thumb for three seconds. Pretty isn’t the goal. Posted is.

On the day, I’m easy to have around. I won’t take over your service, your race or your run sheet, and I won’t make anyone stand still for twenty minutes. I read the room, stay out of the way, and come back with more than you expected — plus straight answers, a price agreed before we start, and your photos back while they still matter.

That’s the whole pitch. If it sounds like the person you want behind the camera, I’d love to hear what you’re planning.
