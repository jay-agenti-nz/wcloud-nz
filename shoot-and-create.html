Why I shoot

I’d be out there with a camera anyway.

Jay D Vera

Most weekends you’ll find me somewhere on the Port Hills — Godley Head, Taylors Mistake, Scarborough Bluffs — running the trails and filming them for my Join me… series. Nobody asked me to make those. I make them because the light on that coast at 7am deserves better than a phone in someone’s pocket.

That’s the whole reason this page exists. I got good at catching the moment where someone’s legs are burning and they’re grinning anyway, and it turns out that’s the same moment that matters at a race finish line, across a café table on a Sunday morning, and in a room full of people who turned up to your launch.

The other half of this is ten-odd years running creative and e-commerce brands. That’s the part that taught me the difference between a photo that looks good and a photo that gets used. When a café owner says “I need something for Instagram this week,” I know what that actually means: frames you can post on a Tuesday without redesigning your whole feed, and a clip that holds a thumb for three seconds. Pretty is not the goal. Posted is the goal.

Photo and video sit alongside the websites I build at White Cloud. Same person, same standard, same phone number.
